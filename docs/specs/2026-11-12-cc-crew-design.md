# cc-crew: design

Written from the plan file when the plugin shipped (2026-11-12), again when the role became a command (2026-11-13), extended when the feedback ledger got its apply side (2026-11-13), and again after the first and the second pass through that ledger (both on 2026-11-13). The decisions and their reasons are the record; the plugin's texts under `plugins/cc-crew/` are what runs.

## Principle

Victor decides what gets built and why, with the chief engineer, in the main session, and the plan fixes the design; the chief engineer answers for the result; every other agent does one bounded job inside the plan, uses its own judgment where the plan is silent or works against its own principle, and never silently: it reports what it decided, what it could not do and what looks wrong.

## What it is for

Victor works with Claude Code on his work repositories through one main session and the agents it starts. Today the way that session should behave is spread over about 25 memories and his global `CLAUDE.md`, builders get hand-written prompts, reviews are run by hand, and plans are long files he does not read. cc-crew turns that into a small team with named jobs, so that a wrong sentence in a plan is caught by someone before it is built, and so that Victor decides from a picture.

## Who judges what

- **Victor (architect):** what gets built and why; approves the picture and the words; ends a point with a stop word; anything paid, outward or irreversible.
- **Chief engineer (main session):** the plan, the briefs, every diff, the push; answers for the result. What follows from the principle but was never said: it acts and reports it under "Decided without asking".
- **Builder (Opus, medium):** its work item. What the plan leaves open is its call. What the principle needs but the plan never said: it does it and reports it first.
- **Helper (Sonnet, or Haiku):** one job for a builder that needs none of the builder's context.
- **Reviewer (Astra; Opus xhigh on a usage limit):** objections on the plan before the first brief and on the combined diff before a push, and the scoring of the wording test. It decides nothing.
- **Tracker (cc-quiet-watch):** watches a source and wakes the chief engineer.

## Decisions

- DEC1 **No coordinator layer.** Design and decisions happen with Victor in the main session; nothing sits between it and the builders. Why: two coordinator sessions built one wrong plan sentence faithfully and agreed on it.
- DEC2 **Decision rights.** Victor's: what the system does, who judges what, anything paid, outward or irreversible. The chief engineer's: how a settled decision is built. In between, it acts and reports under a fixed heading. Why: a design decision taken alone once went unnoticed inside a review.
- DEC3 **The same three classes for a builder.** It builds what the plan decided and owns what the plan leaves open. Where a sentence of the plan would not work or works against the principle, it builds the plain fix and reports it first, or holds that item when the fix is not plain or reaches outside it. A design it merely likes better, it proposes. Why: a builder made thirteen sound calls alone, and built a flawed sentence without questioning it. A wrong call costs one message; silence costs a wrong build.
- DEC4 **Team tone.** Every text speaks to a teammate who owns part of the deliverable, and every rule comes with its reason. Full for a builder and the fallback reviewer, light for a helper, plain for the relay. Why: cold orders make an agent follow the text and stop caring about the whole, which is how patch piles on patch.
- DEC5 **Small edits stay with the chief engineer.** A couple of edits it makes itself; larger work goes to a builder with a brief. Why: an agent with no context does small work worse.
- DEC6 **Stop words.** "settled", "decided", "fechado", "decidido", said about a named point. After one, a new fact is reported once and building goes on as decided. Why: pushback ends on Victor's word and does not restart on a better-sounding argument.
- DEC7 **A fresh pair of eyes.** A reviewer who was not there when the work was made reads the plan before the first brief and the combined diff before a push, from the principle and the plan or diff, never from the chief engineer's conclusions. It is Astra by default, and the Opus fallback when Codex is out of usage. Why: someone who owes the work nothing sees what its author cannot; it raised six objections on a diff whose tests all passed, and seven on this plan. It is framed as fresh eyes and not as another model family, because the fallback is the same family (Victor, 2026-11-12).
- DEC8 **What happens to an objection.** Each claim is checked, then weighed against the whole: would the design built from scratch keep the fix, and is the guard worth the failure it prevents? Confirmed and worth it, inside the chief engineer's rights: fixed and reported. True but not worth its guard: declined, with one line that says why. Victor's, or not refutable with a fact: to Victor, in Astra's words. On a Codex usage limit the fallback reviewer takes the same brief. Why: a review is valid and still narrow. Taken at face value, each objection adds a patch, and patches to one spot pile up into something nobody would design.
- DEC9 **One door to the reviewer.** A script. The chief engineer runs it in the background and reads the answer file; a question about an answer starts a fresh read-only session that reads the earlier exchange. Why: a file cannot be paraphrased. A small relay agent was agreed first and dropped by Victor on the chief engineer's suggestion: it was there to carry follow-ups to the same thread, and Codex cannot resume a thread read-only, so nothing was left for it to do.
- DEC10 **The role is an output style (replaced by DEC32).** It also carries the Concise rules. Why: only one style is active at a time, and a style survives compaction.
- DEC11 **Scope by repository (replaced by DEC32).** One line in a repository's untracked settings file turns the role on; Concise stays the default elsewhere. Why: a parent folder's settings do not reach a child repository, and an untracked file puts nothing in a team repository. Both tested.
- DEC12 **Main-session lines stay in the global CLAUDE.md.** Six lines, under one heading, "Main session only"; each agent text says the section is not for it. Why: six lines do not justify a second style, and Opus and Sonnet subagents skipped such a section in 6 of 6 runs.
- DEC13 **The builder.** Opus, medium effort. One commit per item, only the tests of what it touched, and it never pushes. Why: the chief engineer reads every diff before anything leaves the machine. Medium effort because, in Victor's words, it is the best effort level for builders.
- DEC14 **Helpers.** Sonnet at medium effort, or Haiku for a trivial lookup, started by a builder for work that needs none of its context. They cannot start agents. Allowed, not encouraged. Why: a helper has less context than a builder, and three levels are the most that stay legible. Medium effort for the same reason as for builders (Victor, 2026-11-12).
- DEC15 **Explorers.** The chief engineer may run read-only agents in parallel while planning, without asking. Why: they cannot change anything.
- DEC16 **Parallel builders.** Only for items the plan marks to run together, each in its own worktree, on the branch its brief names. Why: agents of one session otherwise share one writable worktree.
- DEC17 **The size of a plan.** One principle, one picture, one combined diff, one push. The chief engineer asks Victor before splitting, leaning toward one plan. Why: agents underestimate what they can handle.
- DEC18 **One source.** Decisions live in the plan file, and the page and the briefs are made from it. Every change to the file republishes the page, and the page says which version of the plan it shows. Why: a hand-made page went stale within hours because nobody republished it. A habit fixes that; a script that proves the page equal to the file was tried in this plan and dropped as too strict.
- DEC19 **The page.** One picture of the whole, what is new since the grilling, the work in order and what approval starts; decisions already settled are folded away as the record. The look is chosen per plan. Victor says "approved" in the session, and afterwards the page shows each item's state. Why: Victor settled the decisions in the grilling, so rereading them adds nothing. The page is there for alignment; the plan file is for the chief engineer to follow and for the record.
- DEC20 **A brief.** It opens with what the work is for and what the whole plan is for, then the principle, where, the work, the limits with their reasons, and what every brief shares. Its report asks for the builder's own view. Why: explaining the why is what lets a builder catch a wrong sentence.
- DEC21 **Wording is tuned by test.** A cold and a team version of one brief, three runs each on the flawed tracker brief, scored by the reviewer without knowing which wording produced a run. Two more fixtures were planned (a silent gap, a tempting redesign) and skipped by Victor on 2026-11-12: the six runs already showed both behaviours, and more runs would not change what ships; one is added only if the feedback ledger shows a builder stopping needlessly or redesigning. Why: the balance between obedience and freedom cannot be found by argument, and the author of a wording should not grade it.
- DEC22 **Memories.** The posture memories fold into the role text and are deleted, each shown to Victor first, once the trial holds. Why: a rule should have one home, and the role text is always loaded while a memory loads in one project only; on 2026-11-12 a lesson given four times did not hold because it sat in another project's memory.
- DEC23 **Docs.** Decisions live in the plan file; the design file under docs/specs is written from it when the plugin ships. No glossary file. Why: the role text and the agent texts already define the terms, so a glossary would be a second copy that drifts.
- DEC24 **Raw ideas.** An idea Victor throws in mid-turn, said as an idea or opened with "idea:", is checked and refined. It becomes a decision only when he says so. Why: Victor thinks aloud, and an idea built as if it were decided skips his decision and wastes the work.
- DEC25 **Trial.** One work repository first. Undoing it is removing one line. It ran on 2026-11-12, worked, showed how far the line reaches, and led to DEC32. Why: that is where most of the work happens, and a trial that is cheap to undo can start before everything is proven.
- DEC26 **No plan text in the session.** The chief engineer gives Victor the page's link, never the plan's text, and does not use plan mode. Why: Victor trusts the chief engineer with the plan file; what he looks for is alignment, and the page is where that happens.
- DEC27 **When a review stops (its second-fix rule is sharpened by DEC36).** The reviewer labels each objection a blocker (built as written, something fails or the wrong thing gets built) or a note. A pass with no blocker ends the review. A blocker worth fixing is fixed, and the next pass reads only what changed. A second fix to the same spot means the design is in question, not the sentence: the chief engineer zooms out before a third. A third pass that still finds a blocker goes to Victor. Why: a reviewer always finds something, and a plan that keeps breaking has a design problem, not a review problem.
- DEC28 **What approval covers.** Victor's "approved" covers what the page showed him. Anything that is his to decide and was not on the page is not approved, whatever the plan file says. Why: it puts the burden of showing on the chief engineer, with no machinery, and it keeps the page free in form.
- DEC29 **Feedback.** `/cc-crew:feedback`, in any session, writes an entry to a ledger under `~/.claude/.cc-crew/`: the session and time, which piece of cc-crew it concerns, Victor's words, and what he wanted instead (asked once when it is not obvious, or the agent's guess, marked as a guess). When Victor clearly stops or objects to something the crew itself did, the chief engineer asks once whether to file it, and only when it is unmistakable. Entries are weighed and applied by family, one change per cause; "never one patch per complaint" is replaced by DEC34. Why: the role's faults show during real work, far from the plugin's repository, and a ledger applied one entry at a time would rebuild Frankenstein.
- DEC30 **Every decision has a why.** A settled point is recorded: what was decided, by whom, and why. When Victor settles a point without giving the reason, the chief engineer asks for it once. Why: a decision is applied to new cases by its reason, and the chief engineer can only stand behind a ruling it understands.
- DEC31 **Sign-off.** A plan the chief engineer presents carries its signature already: it stands behind the plan, and the plan is ready for Victor's. A plan it is not yet behind is shown as a draft, unsigned, with what it does not agree with. It can withdraw its signature before work starts, and says why. After approval the plan is as much its own as Victor's, and "you decided" is not an answer it gives, also on points it argued against. On the page the two signatures stand side by side, and an approved plan carries a stamp. Why: Victor wants a partner who is accountable for the plan, not an executor of it.
- DEC32 **The role is a command.** `/cc-crew:chief-engineer`, typed by Victor at the start of a session and again after a compaction, puts that one session in the role. The output style, the scope script and the line in one work repository are gone, and Concise stays his style everywhere. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, on his own idea, replacing DEC10 and DEC11. Why: in his words the output style "has its footprints, as we just saw" (its one line reached every `claude -p` and pipeline run started in the repository), it "does not get updated with the upstream, like the concise output style", and "all that I have to do is just invoke the command at the start of the session", about twice a day in sessions that run long. Measured the same night: a style's text is not in the system prompt but in a system message after the first user message, about where a typed skill sits, and a used skill comes back after a compaction.
- DEC33 **No advisor for the chief engineer.** The chief engineer never calls the harness's `advisor` tool. The tool stays on, with Fable as its model, so builders keep it. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13. Why: speed first and cost second, in his words "advising takes quite a bit, and if it adds little, then it's time overhead, on top of the additional cost". For the chief engineer the advisor is its own model rereading the whole transcript, conclusions included, so it is not a fresh pair of eyes, and at the plan and the diff the outside reviewer already is one; a call took a median of 126 seconds and read 204,000 tokens uncached. For a builder on Opus it is a stronger model, "very valuable for the builders probably", which is still a guess. The rule is a line in the role text because the harness switches the advisor per process: `/advisor off` rewrites the default for every session, and the launch flag and the kill-switch variable reach a session's builders too. Opus as the advisor was considered and is refused by the harness, which wants an advisor at least as capable as the main model. Measured: without the line 3 of 3 runs called the advisor, with it 0 of 5.
- DEC34 **Applying feedback.** `/cc-crew:apply-feedback`, typed by Victor in the repository where the crew is built, works through the ledger with him: the open entries grouped by cause, each group brought to him with a recommendation (change, move, wait or decline), and nothing changed before he rules on it. One entry can be enough for a change, and an entry that is not about the crew is discussed like the others and may be moved to a better home, on his yes to the exact text. New wording for the crew's texts ships on his yes to that wording. The ledger is a folder with one file per entry: once what became of an entry is real (a change after its pull request merges, a move after the text is in its new home, a decline at his ruling), the script adds the outcome to the entry's file and moves it from `open/` to `closed/`; one that waits stays open and comes back at the next pass. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, on his own idea. Why: in his words "it can be enough, because it would be discussed in the apply feedback session", and of entries that are not about the crew, "they should be discussed"; the discussion with him is the check that the count of complaints was standing in for. Until then the apply side was one paragraph at the end of the filing skill, with no command to start it, and the ledger had no record of an outcome, so a second pass could not tell an entry already acted on from a new one. The ledger was one file until then; three review passes of the plan each found a new way for a single file, written by many sessions and split back into entries by the look of its lines, to lose, hide or mix up an entry, and a file per entry leaves nothing to split and no file shared between writers. The old file is folded in by the script when it is found, because sessions on an older version of the plugin keep filing there.
- DEC35 **Work is ordered by what depends on what.** The chief engineer lays the steps out as a graph Victor can see, runs side by side everything whose inputs are finished, and runs a cheap, mostly scripted step ahead of the step it depends on, again if that step changes what it checked. Only builders wait their turn (DEC16). A finished commit may get an early read by the reviewer; the read before a push is still of the combined diff (DEC7). On the plan's page the work is drawn as that graph. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, from a ledger entry. Why: in his words, "Quality is above all, but speed also matters a lot. Doing it like this, we don't sacrifice quality for speed." The role text spoke only of sequence, so a serial list was the default and parallel runs came only after he pushed. The early read stays an extra because a review is neither cheap nor a script, and fresh eyes are for the whole change.
- DEC36 **The weighing is asked for where the answer lands.** `review.sh` ends every plan and diff answer file with the crew's reminder: one line per blocker (was the failure ever seen or only constructed, where else does the same mechanism live, would the design built from scratch keep the fix), then fix it, decline it or take it to Victor. From the second read of a change's plan, or of its diff (the same principle in the same output folder, plan and diff counted apart, because a fix to the plan and a fix to the code are not the same spot), it adds that a blocker on a spot already fixed is a stop. The fallback reviewer ends its report with the same reminder, since its answer does not pass through the script. In the role text and the plan skill a second fix to the same spot is a stop: change the design or drop the fix. Victor's global lines on weighing stay as they are. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, from a ledger entry, on his own idea of "some kind of remembering at the right moment". Why: the rule was loaded in three places (his global `CLAUDE.md`, a memory and the role text) and was still skipped, the sixth time in a month. The transcript of that session shows no lookup between a finding and its fix, each fix begun about half a minute after the finding was read, and the only rule that fired was the one tied to a moment and a count. A line that is always in view lost to the finding in hand, so the reminder goes where the finding is read and asks for lines Victor can see. Not measured: Victor dropped the wording test to save usage and watches it in his own sessions. A principle the chief engineer writes on the spot as a guarantee ("exit 0 only when…"), which turns every constructed case into a blocker, waited here for another case and is settled by DEC39; if loops go on with the reminder in place, the next lever is what the reviewer counts as a blocker.
- DEC37 **A stale base is refused.** `review.sh diff` exits 4, before the reviewer runs, when `<base>` has an upstream that the branch left later than it left `<base>`, and names the upstream to pass. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13. Why: worktrees start from the remote and merges happen on GitHub, so a local main is behind after every merge. A review against it handed the reviewer three merged pull requests, cost a pass, and produced the out-of-scope finding that started a patching loop. The script can tell by itself, so nothing is left to remember.
- DEC38 **Old code earns no respect for being there.** When work touches code or a test that a design built from scratch would not have, it is deleted, not built around. A builder deletes what it is confident of and lists it in its report; the rest it proposes to the chief engineer, who decides the same way: confident, it deletes and tells Victor under "Decided without asking"; not confident, it proposes and waits. Confident means it checked why the code is there, the record shows no case that really happened, and nothing else relies on it. Outside what the work touches, it is reported and left. Crew only for now. The reviewer's prompt is unchanged: a deletion it flags is weighed like any finding, and it is the net under a wrong one. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, on his own idea. Why: in his words, agents "try to respect the as-is code", much of it slop or an edge case that never happens and "only bloats the test suite"; "I want my crew to have the freedom to delete code. This, again, is to avoid the Frankenstein pattern." A colleague's code gets no more respect, because "most of the code that is written today is written by AI agents". Confidence rests on a lookup because a feeling is the kind of judgment DEC36 is about.
- DEC39 **A principle written with no plan says what the change is for.** When the chief engineer sends a change to the reviewer and no plan carries an approved principle, it writes one sentence on what the change is for, not a guarantee. The reviewer's prompt, and what it counts as a blocker, stay as they are. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, from a ledger entry, on the chief engineer's recommendation; it ends the wait recorded in DEC36. Why: the case he waited for came within hours. In the review of pull request #104 the principle said "lets through every base it accepted before…", and the reviewer answered with a constructed case (a change and its exact revert), as it had that morning with "exit 0 only when…". The reminder held the second time (declined in one line, no fix, no loop), so the loop is guarded and what was left is the cause: the role text said to give the reviewer the principle and was silent on what that is when no plan exists, while the plan skill tests a plan's principle against the cases discussed. The reviewer's prompt is left alone because the reviewer cannot know whether a failure was ever seen, which is the chief engineer's lookup, and because DEC36 kept that lever for loops that go on with the reminder in place, and they did not.
- DEC40 **Victor is asked in words he holds.** Before the chief engineer asks him to decide, and before a page goes out, every term in it is one he has used or agreed to. The first time another appears, from the code, a teammate, a document or the chief engineer, it comes with where it is from and what it means, and he says yes to the word. It is written where the asking happens: in a grilling a word he has not used or agreed to is put to him first, on a "Terms to agree on" line, and only the questions that need it wait for his answer (in the grilling plugin, so every grilling has it, crew or not; a local change to carry through each sync from upstream), the plan file has a "Terms" section, so the agreed words survive a compaction, and the page shows them under the principle. This is not the glossary DEC23 declined: that would have been a second copy of the crew's own terms, and this is the words of one plan. No line goes into his global `CLAUDE.md`. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-13, from a ledger entry. Why: in his words, "very important to align terminology. the chief should make sure that I understand every term and am comfortable with each one. it is closely related to the acronyms/ids thing." In the session it came from he answered several rounds of a grilling worded in rule ids from a sheet he had not read, names from the code and a label the chief engineer had borrowed from a teammate, then said "im still super confused on the terminology", and the plan's page had gone out before the words were aligned. The role text only forbade words the chief engineer coins, and the memory on explaining labels was loaded and skipped, the sixth correction of this kind since 2026-10-27. As with DEC36, a line always in view lost to the moment, so the terms get a visible place in the round and on the page. Words first, in the grilling: the first wording put the new words and the questions that use them in one round, the reviewer objected that his answers could then settle the decisions without settling the words, and the chief engineer recommended that answering the round count as his yes, since a round for the words costs a round trip. Victor ruled the other way: "I think i want to agree to the words first." He gave no further reason; the chief engineer reads it as the "comfortable with each one" of his entry, a yes he gives and not one implied by an answer. The cost is held down by gathering the expected terms into the first round and by asking in plain words where no term is needed.
- DEC41 **Grilling is the crew's own skill, for Claude Code only.** `/cc-crew:grilling` replaces the thinking plugin's grilling, grill-with-docs and domain-modeling: one skill, whose text is the crew's own, with mattpocock/skills as a source of ideas. In a repo that already has a glossary file the agreed words still go there, and a decision record is offered when a decision earns one. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15, in a grilling held from three reports by chief engineer sessions. Why, in his words: "I don't want to lose precision and direction and conciseness for compatibility"; he works in Claude with the crew more than 95% of the time, and "if that changes, I can simply change my skills." The portable plugin cannot hold a script path, a `claude -p` call or a hook, and the move buys the crew's reviewer, a record in the plan's folder and scripts for rules followed unreliably. This supersedes the plugin merges plan of 2026-11-13, which kept grilling portable because he used it in Codex; Codex now has no grilling. The principle he approved: a grilling brings the user questions they can decide, and tests their answers instead of only collecting them.
- DEC42 **A question arrives ready to be decided.** Research comes before round one (the code, the docs, the web), shown as a "What I checked" block that also says what could not be checked; a question whose fact is unchecked waits. Every question carries something concrete: the session's example, or the real thing when the question is about that. What the thing is for, its cases and its parts come before files and steps. Sure questions come as one block ending in "any objection?"; a leaning one gets its recommendation and one "Would flip it" line, each as its own list item; a coin flip gets its options side by side with their costs. "What a yes starts" appears only when a yes sets off money, someone else's agreement, or something outward or hard to undo. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15. Why: in the reports he decided twice on a fact that was wrong or unread, a question failed until a two-line example settled it, a round reached him as thirteen yes-or-no lines about files, and, in his words, "readability is extremely important". The asker writes no "case against" of its own: four of five such lines repeated the "Would flip it" beside them, and the real one comes from the reviewer.
- DEC43 **Words come as a table, with alternatives, and every round says its new words.** DEC40 stands: a word is agreed before the questions that use it. The table gives the word, where it comes from, what it means and, for a word the asker picked or that reads as tricky, alternatives. A round with no new word prints "New words: none", and the record script prints the agreed words before each round. Words in a repo's existing glossary count as agreed. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15; the alternatives are his idea. Why: a rejected word left him to invent the replacement, two reports saw the terms line skipped or late, and in the grilling that designed this a word slipped past him for two rounds. A line that must be printed cannot be skipped silently.
- DEC44 **A round shows the whole.** A picture of the tree as a mermaid flowchart (settled questions folded, open ones by number, waiting ones pointing from what they wait on) with a count line, and the points set aside, listed until each is settled or dropped. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15. Why: a list hides the root from a detail, and a point "kept in the back of our minds" had nowhere to go.
- DEC45 **Answers are tested.** Each answer that is not plainly safe meets one built case before it counts, and one that breaks comes back under its own number. The crew's reviewer reads the grilling twice, at its first tree and at its close, never every round; its objections are weighed first, so one that a fact settles gets one line and the rest reach Victor as questions in the reviewer's own words. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15. Why: a settled answer that breaks on a made-up case is not settled; a missing question is cheapest early; and a read per round costs a wait and usage each time.
- DEC46 **The record is the plan file.** From round one the grilling writes `~/.claude/plans/<name>/plan.md`: each decision with its why and where else the same mechanism lives, each agreed word, each open question, each point set aside. The plan skill later adds the work to the same file. `record.py` starts the file and checks it: it refuses a decision with no why, a word with no source or meaning, and a question number used twice. Victor decided the record on 2026-11-15; that it is the plan file, and what the script refuses, are the chief engineer's. Why: a compaction then loses nothing, one file cannot drift from a second copy, and each refusal is a failure that was seen.
- DEC47 **The close shows only what Victor did not actively decide.** In order: the points set aside; what was settled by no objection, one line each, as a last call; a decision with no why, asked once; and the principle, proposed there with a built case against each "every", "only" and "never" in it, and read by the reviewer with the decisions. Glossary words are written once, at the close, as one diff, and only words that name things in the project. Then the final picture and a stamp: one "grilled" line with the counts. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15. Why: in one report an absolute sentence was approved and then broken by two reviews after the code was written; in the grilling itself he opened none of six sure items and then asked about one of them; a write per word leaves loose edits and half a glossary if the grilling stops; and the stamp is his taste.
- DEC48 **A small grilling stays small.** A grilling is small when no question in its tree waits on another, however many rounds it takes: the research and the round's format apply, with no picture, no record and no reviewer. A question that comes back because its answer broke does not change that; a new question that waits on another does. A plan that follows a small grilling is written as before, from the conversation. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15, the second half on the reviewer's third read of the plan. Why: two reviewer runs, a record and a picture are more machinery than a one-question subject, and the first wording said both "fits one round" and "from a second round on", which split when an answer breaks.
- DEC49 **The reviewer's door takes the plan file alone.** `review.sh plan`, `grilling` and `diff` take the plan file and read the principle from its `## Principle` section. A grilling whose principle is not proposed yet is read against `## What this is for`, and with no plan `diff` still takes a file that holds one sentence on what the change is for. The answers land in `reviews/` beside that file unless a folder is named. On a usage refusal the script's last line is the folder it prepared, and that folder goes to the fallback reviewer. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15, on the chief engineer's proposal, with no plan and no grilling: he took it from a list of ideas as quick. Why: the principle file was a copy, made by hand for each read, of a sentence the plan file already holds, and DEC18 wants one source; the script took three to five arguments where the plan file names everything. The folder on a refusal is the chief engineer's: with no separate principle file there are no "same two files" left to hand to the fallback, and the folder holds the principle as the script read it.
- DEC50 **A brief reaches its builder as a file.** The chief engineer starts a builder with the path of its brief, not the brief's text, and cuts the briefs again when the plan changes under a builder. Decided by Victor on 2026-11-15, on the chief engineer's proposal, with DEC49. Why: DEC9's reason, a file cannot be paraphrased, holds for a brief as it does for a review, and retyping a brief into a prompt is slow.

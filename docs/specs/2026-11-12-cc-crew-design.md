# cc-crew: design

Written from the plan file when the plugin shipped (2026-11-12). The decisions and their reasons are the record; the plugin's texts under `plugins/cc-crew/` are what runs.

## Principle

Victor decides what gets built and why, with the chief engineer, in the main session, and the plan fixes the design; the chief engineer answers for the result; every other agent does one bounded job inside the plan, uses its own judgment where the plan is silent or works against its own principle, and never silently: it reports what it decided, what it could not do and what looks wrong.

## What it is for

Victor works with Claude Code on his work repositories through one main session and the agents it starts. Today the way that session should behave is spread over about 25 memories and his global `CLAUDE.md`, builders get hand-written prompts, reviews are run by hand, and plans are long files he does not read. cc-crew turns that into a small team with named jobs, so that a wrong sentence in a plan is caught by someone before it is built, and so that Victor decides from a picture.

## Who judges what

- **Victor (architect):** what gets built and why; approves the picture and the words; ends a point with a stop word; anything paid, outward or irreversible.
- **Chief engineer (main session):** the plan, the briefs, every diff, the push; answers for the result. What follows from the principle but was never said: it acts and reports it under "Decided without asking".
- **Builder (Opus, medium):** its work item. What the plan leaves open is its call. What the principle needs but the plan never said: it does it and reports it first.
- **Helper (Sonnet, or Haiku):** one job for a builder that needs none of the builder's context.
- **Reviewer (Astra; Opus xhigh on a usage limit):** objections on the plan before the first brief and on the combined diff before a push, and the scoring of the wording test. It decides nothing.
- **Tracker (cc-quiet-watch):** watches a source and wakes the chief engineer.

## Decisions

- DEC1 **No coordinator layer.** Design and decisions happen with Victor in the main session; nothing sits between it and the builders. Why: two coordinator sessions built one wrong plan sentence faithfully and agreed on it.
- DEC2 **Decision rights.** Victor's: what the system does, who judges what, anything paid, outward or irreversible. The chief engineer's: how a settled decision is built. In between, it acts and reports under a fixed heading. Why: a design decision taken alone once went unnoticed inside a review.
- DEC3 **The same three classes for a builder.** It builds what the plan decided and owns what the plan leaves open. Where a sentence of the plan would not work or works against the principle, it builds the plain fix and reports it first, or holds that item when the fix is not plain or reaches outside it. A design it merely likes better, it proposes. Why: a builder made thirteen sound calls alone, and built a flawed sentence without questioning it. A wrong call costs one message; silence costs a wrong build.
- DEC4 **Team tone.** Every text speaks to a teammate who owns part of the deliverable, and every rule comes with its reason. Full for a builder and the fallback reviewer, light for a helper, plain for the relay. Why: cold orders make an agent follow the text and stop caring about the whole, which is how patch piles on patch.
- DEC5 **Small edits stay with the chief engineer.** A couple of edits it makes itself; larger work goes to a builder with a brief. Why: an agent with no context does small work worse.
- DEC6 **Stop words.** "settled", "decided", "fechado", "decidido", said about a named point. After one, a new fact is reported once and building goes on as decided. Why: pushback ends on Victor's word and does not restart on a better-sounding argument.
- DEC7 **A fresh pair of eyes.** A reviewer who was not there when the work was made reads the plan before the first brief and the combined diff before a push, from the principle and the plan or diff, never from the chief engineer's conclusions. It is Astra by default, and the Opus fallback when Codex is out of usage. Why: someone who owes the work nothing sees what its author cannot; it raised six objections on a diff whose tests all passed, and seven on this plan. It is framed as fresh eyes and not as another model family, because the fallback is the same family (Victor, 2026-11-12).
- DEC8 **What happens to an objection.** Each claim is checked, then weighed against the whole: would the design built from scratch keep the fix, and is the guard worth the failure it prevents? Confirmed and worth it, inside the chief engineer's rights: fixed and reported. True but not worth its guard: declined, with one line that says why. Victor's, or not refutable with a fact: to Victor, in Astra's words. On a Codex usage limit the fallback reviewer takes the same brief. Why: a review is valid and still narrow. Taken at face value, each objection adds a patch, and patches to one spot pile up into something nobody would design.
- DEC9 **One door to the reviewer.** A script. The chief engineer runs it in the background and reads the answer file; a question about an answer starts a fresh read-only session that reads the earlier exchange. Why: a file cannot be paraphrased. A small relay agent was agreed first and dropped by Victor on the chief engineer's suggestion: it was there to carry follow-ups to the same thread, and Codex cannot resume a thread read-only, so nothing was left for it to do.
- DEC10 **The role is an output style.** It also carries the Concise rules. Why: only one style is active at a time, and a style survives compaction.
- DEC11 **Scope by repository.** One line in a repository's untracked settings file turns the role on; Concise stays the default elsewhere. Why: a parent folder's settings do not reach a child repository, and an untracked file puts nothing in a team repository. Both tested.
- DEC12 **Main-session lines stay in the global CLAUDE.md.** Six lines, under one heading, "Main session only"; each agent text says the section is not for it. Why: six lines do not justify a second style, and Opus and Sonnet subagents skipped such a section in 6 of 6 runs.
- DEC13 **The builder.** Opus, medium effort. One commit per item, only the tests of what it touched, and it never pushes. Why: the chief engineer reads every diff before anything leaves the machine. Medium effort because, in Victor's words, it is the best effort level for builders.
- DEC14 **Helpers.** Sonnet at medium effort, or Haiku for a trivial lookup, started by a builder for work that needs none of its context. They cannot start agents. Allowed, not encouraged. Why: a helper has less context than a builder, and three levels are the most that stay legible. Medium effort for the same reason as for builders (Victor, 2026-11-12).
- DEC15 **Explorers.** The chief engineer may run read-only agents in parallel while planning, without asking. Why: they cannot change anything.
- DEC16 **Parallel builders.** Only for items the plan marks to run together, each in its own worktree, on the branch its brief names. Why: agents of one session otherwise share one writable worktree.
- DEC17 **The size of a plan.** One principle, one picture, one combined diff, one push. The chief engineer asks Victor before splitting, leaning toward one plan. Why: agents underestimate what they can handle.
- DEC18 **One source.** Decisions live in the plan file, and the page and the briefs are made from it. Every change to the file republishes the page, and the page says which version of the plan it shows. Why: a hand-made page went stale within hours because nobody republished it. A habit fixes that; a script that proves the page equal to the file was tried in this plan and dropped as too strict.
- DEC19 **The page.** One picture of the whole, what is new since the grilling, the work in order and what approval starts; decisions already settled are folded away as the record. The look is chosen per plan. Victor says "approved" in the session, and afterwards the page shows each item's state. Why: Victor settled the decisions in the grilling, so rereading them adds nothing. The page is there for alignment; the plan file is for the chief engineer to follow and for the record.
- DEC20 **A brief.** It opens with what the work is for and what the whole plan is for, then the principle, where, the work, the limits with their reasons, and what every brief shares. Its report asks for the builder's own view. Why: explaining the why is what lets a builder catch a wrong sentence.
- DEC21 **Wording is tuned by test.** A cold and a team version of one brief, three runs each on the flawed tracker brief, scored by the reviewer without knowing which wording produced a run. Two more fixtures were planned (a silent gap, a tempting redesign) and skipped by Victor on 2026-11-12: the six runs already showed both behaviours, and more runs would not change what ships; one is added only if the feedback ledger shows a builder stopping needlessly or redesigning. Why: the balance between obedience and freedom cannot be found by argument, and the author of a wording should not grade it.
- DEC22 **Memories.** The posture memories fold into the role text and are deleted, each shown to Victor first, once the trial holds. Why: a rule should have one home, and the role text is always loaded while a memory loads in one project only; on 2026-11-12 a lesson given four times did not hold because it sat in another project's memory.
- DEC23 **Docs.** Decisions live in the plan file; the design file under docs/specs is written from it when the plugin ships. No glossary file. Why: the role text and the agent texts already define the terms, so a glossary would be a second copy that drifts.
- DEC24 **Raw ideas.** An idea Victor throws in mid-turn, said as an idea or opened with "idea:", is checked and refined. It becomes a decision only when he says so. Why: Victor thinks aloud, and an idea built as if it were decided skips his decision and wastes the work.
- DEC25 **Trial.** One work repository first. Undoing it is removing one line. Why: that is where most of the work happens, and a trial that is cheap to undo can start before everything is proven.
- DEC26 **No plan text in the session.** The chief engineer gives Victor the page's link, never the plan's text, and does not use plan mode. Why: Victor trusts the chief engineer with the plan file; what he looks for is alignment, and the page is where that happens.
- DEC27 **When a review stops.** The reviewer labels each objection a blocker (built as written, something fails or the wrong thing gets built) or a note. A pass with no blocker ends the review. A blocker worth fixing is fixed, and the next pass reads only what changed. A second fix to the same spot means the design is in question, not the sentence: the chief engineer zooms out before a third. A third pass that still finds a blocker goes to Victor. Why: a reviewer always finds something, and a plan that keeps breaking has a design problem, not a review problem.
- DEC28 **What approval covers.** Victor's "approved" covers what the page showed him. Anything that is his to decide and was not on the page is not approved, whatever the plan file says. Why: it puts the burden of showing on the chief engineer, with no machinery, and it keeps the page free in form.
- DEC29 **Feedback.** `/cc-crew:feedback`, in any session, writes an entry to a ledger under `~/.claude/.cc-crew/`: the session and time, which piece of cc-crew it concerns, Victor's words, and what he wanted instead (asked once when it is not obvious, or the agent's guess, marked as a guess). When Victor clearly stops or objects to something the crew itself did, the chief engineer asks once whether to file it, and only when it is unmistakable. Entries are weighed and applied by family, never one patch per complaint. Why: the role's faults show during real work, far from the plugin's repository, and a ledger applied one entry at a time would rebuild Frankenstein.
- DEC30 **Every decision has a why.** A settled point is recorded: what was decided, by whom, and why. When Victor settles a point without giving the reason, the chief engineer asks for it once. Why: a decision is applied to new cases by its reason, and the chief engineer can only stand behind a ruling it understands.
- DEC31 **Sign-off.** A plan the chief engineer presents carries its signature already: it stands behind the plan, and the plan is ready for Victor's. A plan it is not yet behind is shown as a draft, unsigned, with what it does not agree with. It can withdraw its signature before work starts, and says why. After approval the plan is as much its own as Victor's, and "you decided" is not an answer it gives, also on points it argued against. On the page the two signatures stand side by side, and an approved plan carries a stamp. Why: Victor wants a partner who is accountable for the plan, not an executor of it.

## Tried and dropped

- **A script that proved the plan's page equal to the plan file.** Three review rounds each patched it, and each patch moved the hole. It guarded the folded record nobody reads and could not guard the picture and the highlights, which are the chief engineer's own words. Replaced by a habit (republish on every change, show the plan's version) and by the rule that approval covers what the page showed.
- **A relay agent between the chief engineer and the reviewer.** It was there to carry follow-ups to the same Codex thread. `codex exec resume` cannot be forced read-only, so a follow-up is a fresh session that reads the earlier exchange, and the script does that alone.
- **Two more wording-test fixtures** (a silent gap, a tempting redesign). The six runs on the flawed brief already showed both behaviours.

## Measured

Rows 38 to 43 of `docs/experiments.md`: where settings resolve, a plugin style chosen by name, "Main session only" by model, a helper that cannot start agents, the wording test, and Codex's resume. The wording test's briefs and its scoring prompt are in `evals/cc-crew/`.

## What the builders suggested

What builders proposed, and what was done with each, with the reason.

- **A brief should carry what the whole plan is for** (brief cutter's builder). Taken: a builder who knows what the whole is for is the one who catches a wrong sentence.
- **Print each brief block's line numbers in the plan** (brief cutter's builder). Not now: the script copies lines as written and its tests hold that; worth adding if briefs ever drift.
- **Read Codex's JSON events for the session id and the usage refusal** (review script's builder). Not now: the event names could not be confirmed offline, and a misread refusal only ends in the log being shown; revisit if a real refusal is ever misread.
- **`git archive` leaves out paths marked export-ignore** (review script's builder). Noted: this repository does not use it.

Found while building: `codex exec resume` has no sandbox option, so a follow-up is a fresh read-only session that reads the earlier question and answer as files, not the same thread.
